## 1. Verdict

None of the four sets is buildable as drawn, and none was meant to be: each is a one-floor Remplanner layout with no site plan, north arrow, foundation, roof, section, elevation, electrical or mechanical sheet. There are two Blockers. First, the Alternative is probably too big for Part 9 (the houses-and-small-buildings part of the code): its printed net areas, house plus attached garage, already reach 559.8 m², and outside walls adding more than 7.2% push the building area past the 600 m² Part 9 limit (ABC 2023 1.3.3.3.(1)). Every reasonable wall allowance gives 616–672 m². If it is confirmed, Part 3 governs the whole building, 9.36 gives way to NECB, and every Part 9 table the other specialists used for ALT stops applying. Only the Code wave caught this (CODE-01). Second, Version 1-2, Version 2 and Version 3 print a 120 in (10 ft) ceiling. That puts floor-to-floor above the 3 m limit for a prescriptive ICF basement wall under ABC 2023 9.15.1.1.(1), so the foundation the household assumes becomes an engineered design (STR-01). Version 2 and Version 3 are also within 7–13 m² of the 600 m² line at a 20% wall allowance (CODE-02); Version 1-2 is clear by 70 m² or more. Below the Blockers, the seven specialists filed 124 rows: 2 Blocker, 66 Should fix and 56 Consider. Four Code rows confirm earlier findings rather than adding new ones. All 103 code citations reproduced; none was bounced. On Kayla's own notes the four plans are closer than the layouts look. Version 2 has the most items met (62 of 164). Version 1-2 and Alternative tie on deal-breaker groups met (7 of 11 scoreable). Alternative contradicts the fewest items (7) and misses the most (68). The two closed-kitchen versions (2 and 3) each break a deal-breaker the original does not: the garage entry has no door to the hall, so every trip from the garage to the bedrooms crosses the kitchen (K-330). Version 3 also puts the main living room on no exterior wall, with no window. The Alternative is a different and larger house (+28% heated area) with the simplest outline, four bedrooms and two guest ensuites. It loses the vestibule, the Costco door, any bathroom on the living side and windows in the dining and breakfast rooms. It also has the longest plumbing and duct runs, the biggest spans, and now the Part 9 size problem. The coordinator's ranking is in §10; the choice stays with Jordan and Kayla.

## 10. Coordinator's ranking (a recommendation, not a decision)

Ranked by: deal-breakers kept, items contradicted, Blockers and Should-fix items unique to the plan, and cost drivers. The choice stays with Jordan and Kayla.

1. **Version 1-2 as the base to edit.** It is the cheapest on every driver the review could rank (area, volume, perimeter, plumbing spread, septic flow, routes), it keeps the Costco door and the garage-to-hall door, and it ties ALT on deal-breakers met. Its faults are fixable inside its outline: hall share, laundry door on the public hall, master closets through the bath, closets on exterior walls, and the open kitchen/dining that Kayla dislikes. Closing the kitchen the V2 way while keeping V1-2's mudroom-to-hall door would recover most of what V2 set out to do without ARC-01. Its Blocker (STR-01) is shared with V2 and V3 and is a ceiling-height decision, not a layout one.
2. **Alternative, if four bedrooms, two guest ensuites and a library are wanted, and only after the Part 9 question is settled.** As drawn it is probably over the 600 m² Part 9 limit (CODE-01), so it needs either a trim of roughly 5–12% of gross area, a firewall between house and garage, or a Part 3 design. Any of these changes the plan that was reviewed. It contradicts the fewest notes (7), has the simplest outline, the best master suite against Kayla's notes and the shortest routes, and its basement wall per heated ft² is the lowest. Against it: +28% area, no living-side bathroom, windowless dining and breakfast rooms, exterior-wall tubs, the longest plumbing and duct runs, the biggest spans, no vestibule, and 68 items missing. Fixing the living-side bath and the windowless rooms means moving walls in the main block.
3. **Version 2.** Within a few m² of the Part 9 line at a 20% wall allowance (CODE-02). Highest IN count (62) and the closed dining Kayla asked for, but it breaks K-330 (kitchen as the only route from the garage), loses the Costco door and the sunroom, adds the most corners, the largest terrace, the most fixture units and a glazed bearing wall. One hall door and one pantry door fix the two route findings; the cost drivers remain.
4. **Version 3.** A windowless, exterior-wall-less living room that is also the main through-route, plus the same garage-to-kitchen highway as V2, with the largest heated volume and a 55 ft plumbing gap. Its one advantage, the simplest outline of the three originals, does not offset those.

**Bottom line of the comparison:** Versions 2 and 3 each give up two of Kayla's deal-breakers (garage route through the kitchen; and in V3, daylight in the living room) to get the closed kitchen, and both add 12–14% to the heated area. The Alternative is not a simplified version of the same house; it is a larger, differently organised house whose "simplified envelope" is real on corner count and perimeter-per-area but not on spans, plumbing length or total size. Its total size probably takes it out of Part 9 altogether. Version 1-2 is the only set with clear room under the Part 9 limit, so it is the only one that can grow (a roofed porch, a bigger garage) without approaching the line.
